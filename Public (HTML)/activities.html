Entries use the same fields as a session-plan Activities entry (Coaching Points, Setup, Description, Progressions, Adaptations, Video where available) so they can be copied straight in.

When building a new session, check here first for something reusable before inventing a new drill from scratch.

Game adaptations for contact-level

Quick reference for picking a game to match the session's contact-level target (see claude.html for the Thursday contact-light / Sunday guidance). Full detail for each is in Game-zone below.

Contact-level ladder (low to high):

  1. Touch Rugby — standard touch, any touch ends the carry.
  2. Pop-Up Touch — ball must be popped up to a support player on a touch, not passed.
  3. Ruckly-Touch — as Pop-Up Touch, but the ruck area is actively contested (defence can compete for the ball, not just back off).
  4. Full Contact — as per the laws of the game.

Other coaching variations (layered on top of whichever contact level is in use that day):

  • Fiji Touch — first touch play-on, second touch on the same carrier is a turnover.
  • N-tackle turnover ("Turnover Touch") — turnover after a set number of touches.
  • Line-break Touch — extra steps allowed on a "LINEBREAK" call.
  • Springbok Touch — turnover if the first receiver is caught in contact.

Warm-up

Zoom-Cruise-Boom

Focus: Defence (line-speed)
Setup Ruck shields.
Description A line-speed movement warm-up in three parts: "zoom" (fast approach), "cruise" (settle, stay on toes), "boom" (contact/finish on the shield).
Coaching Points Focus on the zoom (line-speed) and the cruise (staying on toes) — don't worry too much about the boom itself.
Adaptations Run with whatever group split fits the numbers out that day (e.g. several teams rotating through, some in the game-zone / some in a skill-zone).

Rainbow Passing

Focus: Handling
Setup Coloured cones set out in two parallel lines, matched colour opposite colour (e.g. a red cone on one side directly opposite a red cone on the other).
Description Players pass across the gap between the two lines of cones. The coach calls a colour, and players move to (or pass across) that colour's pair of cones — varying the width of the passing gap on the call.
Coaching Points (not detailed in the source notes)
Adaptations Vary which colour is called (and how far apart that pair of cones is) to control the passing distance/width on the fly.

Crazy Passing

Focus: Handling
Setup Players in three lines (A, B, C) spaced out for passing.
Description Ball zig-zags down the lines: A1 passes to B1, B1 to C1, C1 to B2, B2 to A2, A2 to B3, and so on — each player filling in at the back of the line they just passed to (or received into) once they've exited. For "crazy," add a second ball into the pattern at the same time.
Coaching Points General handling cues — elbow up, hands out, catch early, etc. — plus communication and awareness (especially once a second ball is added).
Adaptations Good for warming hands up and waking players up early in a session; add the second ball once the basic pattern is running smoothly to raise the awareness demand.

Game-zone

Fiji Touch

Focus: Continuity
Description Touch rugby adaptation — the first time a ball-carrier is touched, play continues; a second touch on the same player before they've offloaded is a turnover.
Coaching Points Teaches players to support the ball-carrier and to look for support themselves, since a turnover only comes from being caught a second time. High-activity and well-liked — good return for the skill work involved.
Progressions Add a distance-limit or a time-limit on how quickly the ball must be offloaded/moved after the first touch.

Pop-up Touch

Focus: Continuity / Contact-offload
Description Touch rugby adaptation — on a touch, the ball must be popped up off the floor to a supporting player (rather than just passed).
Coaching Points Support — being there for the pop. Communication. Eye contact with the arriving support player before popping the ball — don't pop blind.
Progressions - Add a time-limit to how quickly the ball must be popped.
- Introduce a turnover if the ball isn't moved on within a set time (e.g. ~1 second).
- Step up into the dedicated Offload Technique skill-zone (below) — treat this game as the live application of that skill-zone's work.
Adaptations A good interim step before introducing something more specialised (e.g. the Offload Technique skill-zone) — manage the contact level here rather than needing a separate skill zone first.

Ruckly-Touch

Focus: Continuity / Contact-offload — the contested step up from Pop-up Touch
Description As Pop-up Touch, but the "ruck" area on the floor is actively contested by the defence rather than the ball being handed straight back — defence can compete for it.
Coaching Points Same as Pop-up Touch (support, communication, eye contact before the pop), plus urgency to secure and present the ball quickly now defence can contest it.
Adaptations The step between Pop-up Touch and Full Contact on the ladder above — use when the group's ready for contested-ball pressure but not full tackling yet.

Turnover Touch

Focus: Continuity and Defence — the same base game, tuned two different ways (see Adaptations)
Description Touch rugby with a turnover-after-N-touches rule.
Adaptations This game has been run two different ways for two different objectives — tune whichever fits the day's focus:
- Continuity version: reduce the turnover limit to a low number (e.g. 3 touches) — forces the attack to move the ball rather than get repeatedly caught in contact. Progression: run the same idea in (limited) contact.
- Defence / line-speed version ("Linespeed Turnover Touch"): condition that if the defence gets to the ball-carrier within a set time (e.g. 2 seconds) on a set touch count (e.g. the 3rd), the defence wins a turnover. Tune the touch-count and time-limit to the group. Progressions: penalise the defence for being offside; restrict who's allowed to talk (e.g. only the fullback) to make the value of communication obvious by taking it away.

Springbok Touch

Focus: Defence (line-speed)
Description Touch rugby with the condition that if the defence catches the first receiver still in contact (i.e. before they've passed the ball on), it's a turnover. Named for the Springboks' rush/blitz defence — see playbook.html's Bang call.
Coaching Points Rewards fast line-speed and pressure on the ball straight from source, rather than only rewarding tackles further out.
Progressions Combine with the Blitz Defence (4v3 shape drill) below once the group has the idea.

Line-break Touch

Focus: Continuity
Description Touch, with the adaptation that when the coach/ref calls "LINEBREAK," the ball-carrier may take up to 3 steps before offloading; if there's no support available, they must go to the floor and recycle.
Coaching Points Support timing — being in the right place to receive the extra-steps offload. Communication.

Turnover Contact

Focus: Continuity
Description The contact version of Turnover Touch (above) — same turnover-after-N-touches idea, played with (limited) tackling instead of touch.
Adaptations Keep the tackle count/turnover trigger tight while introducing contact, rather than opening it up to a fully live contested game straight away.

Continuous Defence

Focus: Defence
Description A team stands in the middle and repeatedly defends attacks coming from alternating directions, resetting each time.
Coaching Points Quick reorganisation and communication under repeated pressure from different angles.
Adaptations Works well as a bookend either side of a skill-zone (e.g. run it, break off into a skill-zone, then come back to it to finish) rather than only once in the session.

Skill-zone

2 vs 1 (with offload)

Focus: Continuity
Description Standard 2-vs-1, with the condition that the ball must be moved via an offload rather than a standard pass.
Coaching Points Looking for support (communication). Timing and depth needed to receive an offload — players usually stand far too shallow.
Progressions - Add contact (makes it more chaotic and demands even more depth from the support player).
- Add randomness (e.g. gates to run through) to vary the picture each rep.

Offload Technique (progressive skill-zone)

Focus: Contact / offload
Setup Ruck shield(s) / tackle pad(s) — a shield can make the kneeling stage more comfortable.
Description A staged progression for offload technique: start on knees (falling forward into the offload), progress to running at a tackle pad and offloading round the back of it, then progress further to running between a pair of pads while looking for the offload.
Coaching Points Eye contact and communication with the receiver. Hands free to give the offload (don't get caught one-armed). Lift the ball for your team-mate rather than leaving them to dig for it. If in doubt, keep the ball and stay active on the floor ("pencil") rather than forcing a risky offload. Avoid a double recycle.
Progressions - Offload with both hands, not just the stronger side.
- Introduce other offload types — cat-flap, and round-the-body.

Tackle Diamond

Focus: Defence
Description A tackle-technique skill-zone drill run in a diamond-shaped setup (exact grid dimensions weren't captured in the original notes — worth fleshing out next time this is run).
Coaching Points Defence "wins" the rep if the tackle is completed over the halfway point of the diamond. Technique cues used alongside it: low — chop!, shoulder contact, squeeze.

Continuous Attack with Conditioned Defence

Focus: Defence / Attack
Description Continuous attacking phases against a defence operating under a set condition.
Adaptations Toggle the defence's condition to dial the difficulty up or down — passive (defence gives token resistance, attack focuses on shape/execution) or pressure (defence competes properly, forcing quicker decisions).

Combined game- and skill-zone

Blitz Defence (4 v 3 shape drill)

Focus: Defence
Setup Big pitch / wide channel; 4 attackers vs 3 defenders.
Description Touch progressing to a "simulated-ruck" touch (a touch variant where a tackle is simulated and the ball recycled, rather than just popped). Within that, a skill-zone (max ~10 min) runs the 4v3, with defence coached to blitz up and cut off the wide options, before going back into the main touch game.
Coaching Points Number up. Call your man. Outside shoulder.
Progressions Award defence a turnover for getting off the line and to the ball fast. Overload the attack (add an extra attacker) to force the defence to communicate even more to cope.
Adaptations Can finish the session with a version of this in (limited) contact once the shape is understood.

General structuring & delivery ideas

Not standalone activities, but reusable ways of structuring a session that came out of the same notes:

  • Fixed rotation split: split the squad into groups (e.g. 3 groups of 10) — most stay in the game-zone while one group rotates into the skill-zone, swapping through so everyone gets both.
  • Keep the game-zone running, pull a small number out for a skill-zone: rather than stopping the whole session for a skill fix, keep the main game going continuously and pull a handful of players (e.g. 3) out for a skill-zone rep, then straight back in.
  • Scenario problems dropped into a live game: e.g. "defending your own 5m," "defensive scrum on the 22," "attacking scrum 10m out" — presented as a problem for the players to solve themselves rather than a lecture. Keep any coach intervention very short (~30 seconds) and from one voice, to avoid it becoming talky and dull.

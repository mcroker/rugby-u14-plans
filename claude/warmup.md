# The standard warm-up

Every session opens with the same **five-minute warm-up, led by one of the players** (see `coaching.md` and `age-group.md`). This page is the model: the same four blocks, in the same order, every single time.

It is deliberately built around **switching the brain on, not just the body** — eyes up, talking, reacting, and organising as a line. Those are the things we are actually short of in the first ten minutes of a game, and they are the things a 13-year-old can lead without a coach standing over them.

**Because it is a mental warm-up, it does not prepare anyone for contact.** Before any contact work — tackle technique, scrum engage, contact games — run the separate **two-minute contact warm-up** in `coaching.md`. The two are not interchangeable.

## Why the same thing every week

- **The leader can actually lead it.** A fixed sequence can be memorised. A different warm-up each week means a player reading off a card, badly.
- **It is predictable.** Several of our players do much better when they know exactly what is coming next — same order, same words, every session.
- **It gets better, not just repeated.** Because nobody is learning the format, the group can push the quality: louder calls, faster reactions, tidier lines.

## The five-minute switch-on

Four blocks of about a minute each, then thirty seconds of the leader's own choice.

| # | Block | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Names** | 60s | Squad jogs in a loose box with two or three balls in. Every pass is announced: passer calls the receiver's **name**, receiver calls for it first. **No silent passes.** |
| 2 | **Eyes up (CTC)** | 60s | Keep moving. Leader calls **"CTC!"** — everyone runs the scan from the playbook: **C**rossbar (eyes up), **T**ouchline (scan both), **C**ommunicate. Then the leader holds up a number of fingers or points somewhere, and the squad calls back what they saw. |
| 3 | **React** | 60s | Leader calls one of four words; squad responds instantly, then resets.<br>**"Up!"** — three hard steps forward.<br>**"Back!"** — shuffle backwards, stay square.<br>**"Down!"** — hand to the ground and back up.<br>**"Set!"** — freeze low: feet apart, knees bent, chest up, eyes up. |
| 4 | **Line up** | 60s | Squad scatters. On **"Line!"** they form one defensive line — evenly spaced, numbering off out loud from one end. Reset and repeat three or four times, scattering wider each time. |
| 5 | **Leader's choice** | 30s | Whatever the leader wants to add. Their session, their call. |

## What the leader actually says

The leader needs about six words. This is the whole script:

| Block | Say this |
|---|---|
| Start | *"Warm-up — in the box, two balls, no silent passes."* |
| 1 → 2 | *"CTC!"* (repeat it every ten seconds or so) |
| 2 → 3 | *"Reacting now — up, back, down, set."* Then just call them. |
| 3 → 4 | *"Scatter... Line!"* |
| 5 | *"My choice — we're doing..."* |
| Finish | *"Warm-up done, over to the coaches."* |

## Coaching notes

- **Pick next week's leader at the end of this week's session**, not on the day. It gives them a week to think about their thirty seconds, and it removes the surprise for anyone who would rather not be volunteered on the spot.
- **Let it be led badly before you step in.** The point is the player leading, not a perfect warm-up. Step in only for safety or if the group has genuinely stopped.
- **Quality targets to push over a block:** louder and earlier calls in block 1; genuine head movement rather than a token glance in block 2; no delay between the call and the movement in block 3; straighter, better-spaced lines in block 4.
- **If numbers are low**, shrink the box rather than dropping a block. All four still work with eight players.
- **If it is cold**, run block 3 first and block 1 second — reacting moves people more than passing does.

# The standard warm-up

Every session opens with the same **five-minute warm-up, led by one of the players** (see `coaching.md` and `age-group.md`). This page is the model: the same four phases, in the same order, every single time.

Everything is run **in lines off the try-line**, working out to a cone line (the 22, or ~20m) and back. One shape, four phases, progressively harder — raise the heart rate, add retreating, open the joints up, then finish on the pads.

**Kit:** a cone line at ~20m, and **ruck shields** for the last phase.

## Why the same thing every week

- **The leader can actually lead it.** A fixed sequence can be memorised. A different warm-up each week means a player reading off a card, badly.
- **It is predictable.** Several of our players do much better when they know exactly what is coming next — same lines, same order, same words, every session.
- **It gets better, not just repeated.** Because nobody is learning the format, the group can push the quality: sharper lines, better positions, more effort on the last rep than the first.

## The four phases

Squad splits into **three or four lines on the try-line**. Every rep is out to the cones and back. Roughly five minutes; stretch it to seven or eight if it is cold or there is time.

| # | Phase | Time | Reps (out and back) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heart rate** | ~60s | Jog out, jog back.<br>Jog out, stride back.<br>Stride out, jog back.<br>*Target: breathing hard enough to notice. Not gassed.* |
| 2 | **Jog and retreat** | ~60s | Jog out, **backpedal** back — chest up, eyes up, stay square.<br>Side-shuffle out (change lead halfway), backpedal back.<br>Jog out, backpedal back, **turning on the leader's call**. |
| 3 | **Dynamic stretching** *(Activate style)* | ~2 min | Knee hug out / heel flicks back.<br>Lunge with a rotation out / straight-leg kicks back.<br>Open the gate out / close the gate back.<br>High knees out / skip back.<br>*Controlled and full-range. This is not a race — the line that finishes first has usually done it worst.* |
| 4 | **Pads — Zoom-Cruise-Boom** | ~90s | Shields at the cone line. Each player: **zoom** (fast approach), **cruise** (settle, stay on toes), **boom** (finish on the shield). Two or three each. See `activities.md`. |

**Phase 4 is a shield, not a tackle.** It warms the body up for contact; it does not replace the **two-minute contact warm-up** in `coaching.md`, which still runs before any tackling.

## What the leader actually says

The leader needs about eight lines. This is the whole script:

| Phase | Say this |
|---|---|
| Start | *"Warm-up — three lines on the try-line, out to the cones and back."* |
| 1 | *"Jog out, jog back... jog out, stride back... stride out, jog back."* |
| 2 | *"Now backpedal coming home — chest up, eyes up."* Then: *"Turn!"* on the way back. |
| 3 | *"Stretching now — knee hug out, heels back."* Then call each pair as it comes. |
| 4 | *"Shields up — zoom, cruise, boom. Two each."* |
| Finish | *"Warm-up done, over to the coaches."* |

## Coaching notes

- **Pick next week's leader at the end of this week's session**, not on the day. It gives them a week to think about it, and it removes the surprise for anyone who would rather not be volunteered on the spot.
- **Let it be led badly before you step in.** The point is the player leading, not a perfect warm-up. Step in only for safety, or if the group has genuinely stopped.
- **Phase 3 is the one that gets rushed.** It is also the one doing the injury-prevention work — the RFU's **Activate** programme is the reference if you want the full version. Slow it down; call the movement rather than letting the lines race.
- **Quality targets to push over a block:** straighter lines and a proper start together in phase 1; genuinely square, chest-up retreating in phase 2; full range in phase 3; a real change of pace between zoom and cruise in phase 4.
- **If numbers are low**, use two lines rather than dropping a phase. All four work with eight players.
- **If it is cold**, add a rep to phase 1 rather than cutting phase 3.

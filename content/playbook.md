# Playbook & Calls

> **This is an empty skeleton — the headings, not the contents.** A playing style is the most team-specific thing a squad has, so no playbook ships filled in. Copy this file into your team folder, delete this note, and write your own.
>
> Keep this page **clean enough to hand to the players**: calls, shapes and moves only. Squad context goes in `age-group.md`, how you coach goes in `coaching.md`, and what you're working on when goes in `blocks.md`.

## Playing philosophy

One or two sentences naming the style you're committing to, in words a player would repeat. Concrete beats aspirational — a target ruck speed says more than "play with tempo".

### Guiding principles

A short list — five or six at most. **Every session should map back to at least one of these**, and a drill that maps to nothing is a drill you don't need, so keep them few enough to hold in your head and specific enough to test a session against.

## Calls

**Keep calls short, single-word, and used consistently in training so they become automatic in games.** Predictable, consistently-named calls beat ad-hoc instructions under pressure, and they cut the cognitive load for neurodiverse players.

For each call, give the name, when it's used, and what each player does. A diagram earns its place wherever the shape is easier seen than described — put the web-sized image in your team's `images/web/` folder and reference it by filename: `![Name of the shape](shape.png)`.

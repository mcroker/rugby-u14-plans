# Japan's 1-3-2-2 — would it work at U14?

*Discussion note, Sept 2026. Not part of the playbook — nothing here is agreed or being coached.*

## Where this came from

Our guiding principles include *"move the ball wide more often than setting up pods"*, which sits against our
documented default attacking pattern — a **single forward pod of 3 in front of 10**, zig-zag 5m to 5m. That
contradiction is the last open item on the principles.

Japan's **1-3-2-2** shape was raised as a possible answer:
[The 1-3-2-2: how Japan's attacking shape has helped upset the balance](https://ekrugbyanalysis.com/2019/10/14/the-1-3-2-2-how-japans-attacking-shape-has-helped-upset-the-balance-at-this-years-world-cup/).
The eight forwards are pre-distributed across the pitch in four groups — 1 alone on the wing, 3 on the 15m line,
then 2 and 2 — so every channel already has a receiver.

## Verdict: don't adopt the shape. Take one idea from it.

The full shape is beyond this squad this season. That 1+3+2+2 = 8 forwards fits U14 exactly is a coincidence, not
evidence it is coachable.

Against it, for us specifically:

- **It lives on distribution, which is our named weakness.** The value is a pass reaching a pod that is already
  wide — long, flat, off both hands. Run it before that exists and the turnovers just happen further from our
  support.
- **Four pods means four resets every phase**, at pace, under fatigue and contact — not just in a walk-through.
- **It needs all eight forwards.** Thursday turnout is patchy and Sundays split Blue/White, so a full-turnout shape
  gets rehearsed rarely. A shape we practise occasionally is worse than no shape.
- **The article says the shape is not the edge.** "Any team could start playing in this formation tomorrow" —
  Japan's advantage was improvising *within* it. Copied without the skills it produces players standing in channels
  waiting for a ball that never comes, which is the opposite of the continuity we want.
- **It is a team-tactics layer**, and we are deliberately working at individual and small-group skills this block.

## What is within grasp — the idea, not the formation

1. **"Know your channel, be there before the ball."** One habit, no formation. Already being coached in Thursday's
   Attack off lineout — both rucks land in predictable places, so go before the ball does. Most of the value, none
   of the complexity.
2. **One more pre-positioned group, not three.** We already run a pod of 3 in front of 10. Adding a single standing
   group wider — so there is always somebody out there — is one increment on what we have, and it is enough to stop
   width and pods competing.
3. **A lone fast forward staying wide.** One instruction to one player, likely the 6 or 8 already spare at the
   lineout, and the most Japan-like part of the whole thing.

Even these wait on passing range improving. Block 2 at the earliest.

## Why the underlying idea is still worth having

With **one** pod, the forwards have to fold to it every phase, so going wide means abandoning it — width and pods
genuinely compete, which is why the principle and the default pattern read as opposites. Any pre-positioned second
group means width costs a pass, not a fold. If we resolve the open item, the wording would be:

> Pods exist to create width, not to replace it.

Two things already point that way:

- **"Two to the ruck in attack"** is the same idea from the other end — you cannot man wider groups if you
  over-commit at the breakdown.
- **The calls transfer unchanged.** Bull, Tip, Fox, Hippo and Piggy describe what a pod *does*, not where it
  stands — which matters, because we keep the vocabulary short and constant on purpose.

## The thing that gates all of it

Passing. Every version of this idea, including the cheap one, needs a longer, flatter, both-handed pass than we
have now. Which is convenient — passing is a core skill in every session, and Thursday's focus.

## For the coaches to decide

1. Do we want pre-positioned width at all, or stay with the single pod and fold?
2. If yes, is it idea (2) alone — one extra standing group — or more?
3. Not before Block 2 either way. Agreed?

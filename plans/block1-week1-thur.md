## Activities

### Essential skills — passing (Crazy Passing)

**Coaching Points:** elbow up, hands out, catch early; call for it before it comes. Once the second ball goes in, the demand is awareness — knowing where both balls are without stopping to look.

**Setup:** see `activities.md`. Players in three lines (A, B, C), spaced for passing. In a third of a pitch, run two separate sets rather than one long one.

**Description:** the ball zig-zags down the lines, each player filling in behind the line they passed to. Add a second ball once the basic pattern is running cleanly — that is the point of the drill, not an optional extra.

**Progressions:**
- Second ball in.
- Widen the spacing between lines so the pass has to travel.
- Put a time limit on a full circuit and try to beat it.

**Adaptations:**
- If the pattern keeps breaking down, take the second ball out and rebuild rather than letting it stay scrappy.
- Shorten the spacing if passes are dropping short — a pass at full stretch teaches nothing.

### Whole-group intervention — Lineout positioning recap

**Coaching Points:** the same content as Sunday, not new material: where each player stands, the 5m and 15m lines, **uncontested** — no jumping or lifting (see `laws.md`).

**Setup:** whole squad, cones marking 5m and 15m. **Agree beforehand who leads this segment; everyone else holds.** Note individual fixes and take them to those players in the skill zone.

**Description:** a short (3–4 min) recap, visibly quicker than Sunday's. Get them setting up the line themselves rather than being placed.

**Progressions:**
- **If they form the line cleanly straight away, bank the time and move on.**
- If there is time and they are ahead of it, have the thrower call **front, middle or back** before the throw so the line starts thinking about a target. The **B/W/G** calls themselves are still Week 2 — this is the idea, not the vocabulary.

### Main game — Narrow touch, lineout restarts

**Coaching Points:** line-speed off the **Bang** call; spacing and numbering up; the shuffling retreat; talk. In a narrow channel, defence should be winning more than it did on Sunday.

**Setup:** small-sided, in a **narrow channel** — tight enough to favour line-speed and force quick hands.

**Description:** the same game as Sunday, restarting each phase from an uncontested lineout throw — returning to it a second time is the point, with the constraints tightened rather than the game replaced. Defence scores for line-speed; attack scores a bonus for a clean lineout catch leading to a try.

**Progressions:** the conditions below are the session's defensive load — **add them one at a time and leave each one in.** They carry straight through into the next game block, so don't spend them all here.

- Introduce **Bang** as the blitz trigger (see `playbook.md`) — this is its first outing, so expect to stop the game to reset it a few times.
- **Springbok Touch** (see `activities.md`): **if the first receiver is caught with the ball still in hand, it's a turnover** — and the turnover is taken as a **lineout to the defending team**, not just handed over. Every defensive win manufactures another lineout, which is what we want more of tonight.
- Tighten the line-speed scoring window as the game goes on.
- **Second half of the game: Attack off lineout is on**, off every lineout in the opposition half. A point for the first two forwards who reach ruck 2 before the ball is available.

**Adaptations:**
- Narrow the channel further if attack is getting out too easily; widen it a little if defence is smothering everything and the game has stopped flowing.
- If numbers are low, drop to smaller teams and run one game rather than two — a thin game teaches nothing about line-org.
- If **Springbok Touch** is producing a turnover every single play, the channel is too narrow or the attack is standing too flat — fix the game before softening the condition.

### Whole-group intervention — Attack off lineout

This is **5-man Rhino** (see `playbook.md`), walked through and then run.

**Coaching Points:** **both rucks land in predictable places.** Nobody watches the ball and reacts — you already know where you are going, so go, and arrive before the ball does.

**Setup:** whole squad, one lineout set on the 15m line. **Five in the line** (props, locks, 7), **hooker throws**, and the **two spare forwards — typically 6 and 8** — stand in front of 10. Walking pace, no opposition to start.

**Description:**

1. **Lineout** — ball off the top to **9**.
2. **Ruck 1** — 9 to **6 and 8**, who carry. Resourced by them and **12**. Just off the line, every time.
3. **Ruck 2** — locks and 7 fold round and race there. Keep it tight.
4. **Backs attack the open** off ruck 2.
5. **Hooker and props stay behind** — width on the next phase, and they defend the blind.

Walk it twice, then run it at pace. Point at both ruck positions before the ball moves, then get there.

**Progressions:**
- Add a passive defence so the carriers are actually stopped.
- Move the lineout to a different spot and ask where the two rucks land now.
- Straight back into the game, running it off every lineout in the opposition half.

**Notes:** the **B/W/G** calls come in Week 2 — this is the shape, not the calls.

### Whole-group intervention — Defending the lineout

**Coaching Points:** **four jobs, one each — know yours before the throw.** Their maul set-up will be poor, so the two nearest go straight through it rather than holding on to it. Nothing starts until the catcher's feet are back on the ground.

**Setup:** whole squad, straight on from the attacking shape — same lineout, coaches now standing in as the throwing team. Six minutes, walked then run. Keep the whistle in your hand: this is a picture, not a workshop.

**Description:** we can't contest the catch (see `laws.md`), so it all happens after it. Walk the four jobs, then run it twice:

1. **Nearest two in the line — counter-maul.** Drive the receiver the moment it's legal, and **burst through to attack the seams**. Don't settle in and push.
2. **Rest of the line — pressure 9.** Through their line, just legally enough not to be pinged. No clean, unhurried delivery.
3. **Hooker — stay in the channel.** That's the front-peel covered.
4. **9 — at the back**, and with the **tail-gunner, shoot** for the nearest back — or the blind-side winger if he's the threat.

**No contact tonight**, so the counter-maul is walked and then run at touch: the two nearest touch the receiver the instant it's legal and step through the seam, everyone else does their job at full speed. The drive itself waits for Sunday.

**Progressions:**
- Move the throw to a different spot and ask each group what their job is now.
- Have the coaches deliberately set up a scruffy maul and let them find the seam.
- Straight into the game, live off every opposition lineout.

**Notes:** the discipline line matters more than the aggression — a penalty for offside or obstruction hands back everything the pressure won.

### Back into the game

**Coaching Points:** **Both sides of the lineout are on, every time.** With the ball: **Attack off lineout** off every lineout in the opposition half — forwards know where both rucks are before the ball moves. Without it: the four jobs, called before the throw. **Springbok Touch stays on** — the turnover is a lineout to the defence.

**Setup:** same game, same channel. Thirty minutes, so plan on two clear halves with a short reset between them. **Coaches take their bags and spread out around the pitch** before this block starts — not clustered together.

**Description:** the lineout restarts now feed the shape on one side and the defensive picture on the other, rather than just restarting play. This block is where the lineout defence is actually learned — the intervention was six minutes, this is the repetition. Stop and reset if either side of it does not appear; the expectation is that both are used, not that they are available.

**Progressions:** most of the extra time tonight is here, and it goes on defence. **Add these one at a time, in this order, and leave each one in** — the point is that by the end the defence is holding several demands at once.

- **Scanning — the Coach's Bag** (see `activities.md`). Each coach has a bag and holds an item up for a few seconds every half-minute or so. **A try only counts if the scorer can name the last item shown.** This is **CTC** made to cost something (see `playbook.md`) — eyes up, and talk, because nothing stops a team-mate calling it across.
- **Connected touch.** A touch only counts if a second defender is within a step of the man who made it. A dog-leg in the line means the touch doesn't count and the attack plays on — nobody defends on their own.
- **One voice on Bang.** A nominated caller per phase, rotating, so the trigger comes from a person rather than from everyone at once. If a coach hears no call, the attack gets a free play.
- **Silence.** Two minutes with no defensive talk at all, then take it off. They will feel the difference far better than they will be told it.
- Score it: a point for the first two forwards reaching ruck 2 before the ball is available; a point to the defence for a touch on the receiver inside two seconds of the catch.
- **Late in the block:** the defensive team names its four lineout jobs out loud before the throw, then stops naming them — and see whether they still happen.

**Adaptations:**
- **The bag condition is the first thing to drop** if the game has stopped flowing. Everything else here is defence; that one is awareness, and it's the cheapest to lose.
- If the defensive jobs are collapsing into everyone chasing the ball, freeze it at the catch and re-point the four roles rather than talking over the top of a moving game.
- If tries keep being disallowed on the item and the game is dying, let any team-mate answer for the scorer — that turns it straight into a communication drill.
- Rotate who is on the throw so both halves of the squad get repetitions of both sides.

### Hooker throw-in (pull-out skill zone)

**Coaching Points:** the same cues as Sunday — consistent routine, ball back **behind the head**, both hands, step into it, follow through **pointing at the target**.

**Setup:** hookers peel off with a coach during the passing block — ten minutes, then back in for the lineout recap and the whole main game. Mark a 5-man line with cones, receiver at front, middle and back.

**Description:** the step up from Sunday's Cross-Bar Challenge (see `activities.md`) — a real target at a real position rather than the bar. Open with two minutes of the Cross-Bar Challenge at the posts to warm the arm up, then move to the marked line. If our third of the 4G happens to include the posts, run the Cross-Bar Challenge again first as the warm-up for it; if not, two poles at bar height make a serviceable substitute.

**Progressions:**
- Call the target as the hooker starts their routine, so they cannot pre-set.
- Add a defender's hands over the target to force height.

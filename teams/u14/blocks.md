# U14 Rugby — Session Plans

Session-by-session plans, organised by 6-week block (see `coaching.md` for the block-based coaching model and game/skill-zone shapes, `age-group.md` for training days/facilities, squad structure, forwards/backs splits and contested-scrum and match-day rules, and `CLAUDE.md` for general session-planning mechanics — all of which apply across all blocks). Calls and shapes used in drills should match `playbook.md`; any law-specific coaching points should match `laws.md`; actual dates are tracked in `calendar.md`.

## Block 1 (Weeks 1–7)

### Overview & objectives

**Theme: Defence, plus introducing the two critical new U14 skills — lineout and scrum.**

- **Defence:** builds toward our full defensive system — pillars, **Bang** blitz trigger, ruck-defence, lineout-defence (see `playbook.md`'s Defence section, which is the master reference for all of this). This block introduces the pieces individually before combining them: ruck-defence from Week 3, all four pillars together full-team from Week 5. Whether/when to introduce **drift vs. blitz** on top of our default blitz is under review — see `Saved for later blocks` below.
- **Lineout:** positioning (where to stand), throw/catch/pass to 9, the **B/W/G** calls, and — once that's solid — the **5-man Rhino** strike move (2 spare forwards + 12 hit ruck 1, forwards fold for Rhino, backs play). An 8-man lineout is used specifically for catch-and-drive work in Week 5 — driving maul is attacking-22-only (see `playbook.md`).
- **Scrum:** safe setting, tower of power/body position, the back row's role (control the ball at the base), 9–hooker communication on the feed, and **DSP** as the default move off the base. **Live, contested scrum starts from Week 2** — the first week scrum is introduced — since Sunday is when we can field two full packs; Thursday stays non-contested technique work (see `age-group.md`).

(Full detail on all calls and moves above is in `playbook.md`.) Per the **skills pyramid** (see `coaching.md`), Block 1 lives mostly at the individual- and small-group-skill layers, building toward team moves (5-man Rhino, DSP) by Weeks 5–6.

**Block 1 runs Sunday 6 September – Thursday 22 October.** Weeks are grouped **Sunday, then the following Thursday** — e.g. Week 1 is Sun 6 Sep + Thu 10 Sep. **Week 7 was added for defence**, pushing Block 2 back a week.

Every training session carries the **defence** thread plus a forwards-skill focus (lineout or scrum, alternating by week rather than day). Where the forwards are tied up in a set-piece skill zone, the backs get their own parallel focus, kept in line with the block's theme rather than a separate attacking agenda.

**Pace — slowed down after Week 2.** We were going a bit fast. **Blitz and line-speed are the main thread**: they come first in every session, and new material waits until they hold up in a game.

### Outline session plans for each week

#### Week 1 — Sun 6 Sep & Thu 10 Sep (season opener)

**Defence:** individual tackle skills as the technical base (Sun) moving to passing (Thu), with line-org introduced alongside — main game a touch/tag game with a line-speed target; skill zone runs 1-on-1 tackle-technique fixes (controlled, small-group) plus the line-org cues (spacing, numbering up, CTC scanning, shuffling retreat, communication) and the **Bang** blitz-trigger call.

**Forwards-skill focus: lineout — the squad's very first exposure.** Hookers peel off for a mini skill zone on the throw-in (accuracy, grip, release) while everyone else does handling work; a short whole-group intervention covers lineout positioning (where to stand) and the laws (uncontested, no lift — see `laws.md`) before the main game, which restarts each phase from a lineout throw-in. Thursday has no standing positioning block at all — it is reinforced in the lineout restarts of the games and in the forwards breakout — and adds **both sides of the lineout together** — the attacking shape (5-man Rhino) taught as a forwards/backs split — forwards, 9 and 12 on the two rucks, backs on the edge off a midfield ruck — then joined up, and **defending the lineout** (see `playbook.md`) coached entirely inside the games, using the pause every lineout gives you rather than a block of its own. The two games are deliberately different shapes: the first narrow, so line-speed wins, the second wide, because the backs have just been taught edge attack and because width is what exposes a disconnected defensive line. Thursday also runs to 90 minutes (18:45–20:15), and the extra game time is spent on defence: **Springbok Touch** with the turnover taken as a lineout to the defending team, cumulative conditions for connectedness and communication, and the **Coach's Bag** scanning condition (see `activities.md`) as the first live outing for **CTC**.

**Backs-skill focus:** defensive alignment while the forwards unit is tied up in its lineout skill zone — Continuous Defence (see `activities.md`) is a good vehicle for this.

#### Week 2 — Sun 13 Sep & Thu 17 Sep

**Defence:** blitz and line-org continued, main game scoring tightened so both line-speed and communication count.

**Thursday 17 Sep is low contact** — some players will have played that day — and defence leads it: blitz and the attacker's outside shoulder as a 3 v 3, then two small-sided games of Presentation Touch. **The scrum block and exit kicks are dropped** — set-piece lives inside the 30-minute **semi-opposed scenarios game** that closes the session — receive the kick-off first (where the shape gets introduced), then attack from a lineout (**5-man Rhino**) and from a scrum (**DSP**), each walked through before it runs. A controlled Tackle Diamond runs opposite the 3 v 3, the teams swapping halfway — the one piece of contact.

**Forwards-skill focus: scrum — the squad's very first exposure.** Sunday: 8-man setup, the engage sequence (crouch–bind–set), tower of power/body position — **contested from this first week**, since Sunday is when we can field two full packs. Thursday: **no scrum technique block in the end** — set-piece is Rhino and DSP inside the closing scenarios. (We won't reliably have two full 8-man packs on a Thursday anyway — see `age-group.md`.) Sunday, and again in Thursday's DSP scenario: 9 and hooker practise the feed — the hooker taps when he is ready and 9 feeds on the tap, and back-row work on controlling the ball at the base so it doesn't spill loose.

**Backs-skill focus:** split across the two days to spread the load. **Sunday** ties straight into that day's live scrum work — **DSP** as the default attacking move off scrum, plus line-pressure defence from a scrum situation. **Thursday** introduces **kick receipt** instead — catching, the pendulum, and working back to the middle for a fast ruck. **Exit kicks come out of Week 2 and still need a session** (see the carried-forward notes above).

## Block 2 (Weeks 1–7)

### Overview & objectives

**Theme: continuity.** Keep the ball alive — through the hands, through contact, and off the floor in two seconds. Three threads run through every session, each building week on week:

- **Attacking micro-skills** — running lines (**hard line** and **trail line**) and the ball-carrier's decision: carry, pass, or pull back.
- **Forwards — footwork into contact.** Don't just hit the pad. Footwork before contact, then **Tip**, the ball back inside (**Fox**), or a sidestep (see `playbook.md`). Includes **knowing when to concede in the tackle** — forwards fight on their feet too long, which slows the ball and risks the rip. Going to ground at the right moment, and staying active on the ground, is what keeps the ruck to two seconds.
- **Offloads and support play** — the support runner's depth and timing, and the offload itself, through a ladder of touch games from 2 v 1 to Fiji Touch.

**What White showed on Sun 11 Oct:** solid on first phase, lost after several — in attack and defence — and passive at the ruck despite a good rucking drill. Every thread should end in a game that runs to multiple phases, with ruck speed coached inside it.

Tackle technique and passing stay a standing per-session requirement (see `coaching.md`). Handling errors and 1 v 1 tackling are the two work-ons both Round 1 analyses share.
